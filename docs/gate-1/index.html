Three directions for the AI Alchemy site, and a plan to build the strongest one

I went through the current site and its code, Heron AI on desktop and mobile, and 13 recent Awwwards sites in a real browser. This pack shows what I found, three original directions with sketch compositions, a recommended direction with a full homepage storyboard, and the redesign strategy. Nothing has been rebuilt.

Recommendation: Direction 1, “As-Built”

The site presents itself as an engineering drawing of a mid-market company's systems. It opens on the drawing as things stand today, with redline marks on the problems. As the visitor scrolls, the drawing is redrafted revision by revision until it shows one connected operation. The four revisions are your real process: Discover, Architect, Build, Validate. It says “Systems Integration & Modernization” through its form, not only through its copy, and it can be built to a high standard with SVG and GSAP, without a heavy 3D scene.

Decisions

  1. Open: which visual direction goes into Gate 2.
  2. Decided: lead with Systems Integration & Modernization for established mid-market organizations, explained plainly. Architecture is the foundation of delivery.
  3. Decided: GTA is a trust signal (“Based in the Greater Toronto Area”) under the hero, not the proposition. No wider service area until confirmed.
  4. Decided: Products & Innovation shows “Coming soon”.
  5. Still needed: real proof material (client stories, figures, testimonials), so nothing is invented.

Confirmed messaging (David, 23 Nov)

Headline: “Give your business the tools to grow.” Alternate: “Give your business the tools to stay ahead.”

Supporting line: “We replace outdated technology and connect fragmented systems, so your people spend less time on manual tasks and more time moving your business forward.”

Primary action: “Plan Your Modernization”.

Lead offer: Systems Integration & Modernization for established mid-market organizations, explained plainly: connecting disconnected systems, modernizing legacy software, and making CRM, ERP and custom applications work together. Architecture is the foundation of delivery.

Trust line: “Based in the Greater Toronto Area”, under the hero or in the company introduction. Products & Innovation: Coming soon.

The sketches below now use this copy. In As-Built, “architecture is the foundation” is what the drawing itself shows: we draw the plan first, then build to it.

1The current site

ai-alchemy.ca is a hand-written static site on GitHub Pages: 17 pages, self-hosted fonts (Newsreader, Inter Tight, JetBrains Mono), about 110 KB of hand-written CSS and JS for the homepage, no framework and no third-party requests. The homepage, “The Field”, is a fixed canvas of short engraved strokes that gathers into rings as you scroll. A draft pull request (#1, “Orrery”) replaces the hero with a WebGL copper-and-gold instrument.

Worth keeping

  • Plain, accurate copy: in-house in the GTA, a reply within one business day, “you keep approval”, illustrations labelled as illustrations.
  • A real process (Discover, Architect, Build, Validate) and four industry pages with specific problems and fixes.
  • Good accessibility groundwork: skip link, no-JS and reduced-motion states, visible focus, structured data, llms.txt.
  • No trackers and no third-party requests. Fast to host, nothing to patch.
  • The three-step project brief that opens as an email. It works and needs no back end.

What holds it back

  • The look matches what generated design defaults to right now: cream ground, high-contrast serif, terracotta accent, all-caps mono labels, 01/02 numbering. The design skill we now have flags exactly this set. It reads as competent but not ownable.
  • The field is abstract. Nothing on the page shows the actual work (CRM, ERP, integrations, legacy systems), so the visitor has to translate.
  • Messaging is four equal services. The brief puts Systems Integration & Modernization first and introduces two divisions; neither appears on the site.
  • The hero is blank or black for seconds on first load, and the headline is hard to read in that state.
  • 15,200 px of page on desktop, with several pinned stretches. The story is long for a mid-market buyer skimming on a phone.

2Heron AI, the benchmark

Heron (by Bearplus, currently Site of the Day) sells an AI agent that works inside architects' design tools. I went through it at 1440 × 900 and at phone size, scrolling forward to the footer and back. It is built on Webflow with GSAP ScrollTrigger, SplitText, Lenis smooth scroll, Barba page transitions, Swiper and one canvas.

Why it works

  • One visual language from loader to footer. Every element is a technical drawing: hairline grid, dimension ticks, plans, elevations. Nothing is decoration from somewhere else.
  • The subject is the art. The drawings are what the customer works on every day, so the visitor recognises their own world in the first second.
  • Strict hierarchy. Huge condensed caps headline, small mono metadata, generous empty paper. One accent colour (red) used only to mean “the agent is here”.
  • Scroll as construction. The plan is built as you scroll, then annotated. Reverse scroll un-builds it cleanly. It is choreography, not a stack of fade-ins.
  • Pacing. Dense sections alternate with single-phrase pauses.

What we take, and what we don't

  • Take: drawing a subject from the buyer's own world; one accent with one meaning; building the key visual on scroll and keeping it reversible; a finale that returns to the opening motif.
  • Leave: the long loader (we want the headline visible at once); scroll-jacked smooth scrolling on touch; architecture imagery, the orange circle and the all-caps grotesk, which are Heron's identity.
  • Note: its body text is small and low-contrast in places, and several sections need scrolling to read at all. We can match the craft and beat it on readability.

4Three original directions

Each direction starts from something true about systems integration work. The sketches are compositions at real proportions (desktop 1440 × 900 and phone 390 × 844) to judge layout, type and colour, not finished art. Headlines are working copy for review.

1 · As-Built

RecommendedSVG + GSAP

Idea. Engineers call the drawing of a system as it actually exists “as-built”. Every mid-market company has one in its head: the CRM that doesn't talk to the ERP, the nightly CSV, the Access database one person understands. The site draws that, marks the problems in redline, then redrafts it in revisions until it is one connected operation. The visitor sees their own mess, then sees it fixed, which is the whole pitch for Systems Integration & Modernization.

Tokens

Vellum #E8ECEB Ink #15191D Draft cobalt #2147C9 Redline #D7322B Pencil #7B848A Night sheet #1A1F24
DisplayArchivo Condensed
TextIBM Plex Sans 17/27
AnnotationIBM Plex Mono 12–13

Redline means a problem in your systems, and only that. Cobalt means our work. Both open-source (OFL), self-hosted.

Signature moment

The redraft. One pinned sequence, scrubbed by scroll: tangled connectors straighten onto an integration layer, the redline clouds are struck through, replaced boxes swap in, and the revision letter steps A, B, C, D. Scrolling back un-drafts it exactly.

Motion and 3D

Line work draws with stroke animation; annotations write on as plain text; sheets slide like a drawing set. No 3D scene is needed. An optional isometric “exploded view” of a system can be added with SVG transforms. Reduced motion shows Rev A and Rev D side by side.

Why it fits

Mid-market operations and IT leads recognise their own stack in the first second. It shows the method (drawn before built, approved at each revision) that the current copy only describes. It is precise, calm and credible, which suits a firm asking to touch core systems.

Mood references

2 · Patch Bay

InteractiveSVG + Canvas

Idea. Integration is patching: a signal leaves one system and arrives in another. The homepage is a rack panel of jacks labelled with the systems a business runs. Visitors drag a cable from CRM to ERP and watch an order travel through; a status light turns from amber to green. Each service is a different kind of patch. It is tactile and memorable, and it lets a visitor do the work for a second instead of reading about it.

Aluminium #D3D6D9 Rack #141618 Sales cable #F07A22 Operations cable #2D6CDF Finance cable #F2C230 Live LED #39D07A
DisplayChivo ExtraBold
LabelsChivo Mono, like engraved panel legends

Signature moment

The first patch. A cable follows the pointer or finger with real slack (a short rope simulation), clicks into the jack, and a small order card travels the cable into the ERP. Keyboard users pick two jacks from a list; screen readers hear “CRM connected to ERP”.

Motion and 3D

Physics on cables only, Canvas 2D or SVG. Services scroll by as panels in a rack, each with its own patch. Light 2.5D depth on the rack; no WebGL needed. Reduced motion shows the patched state, no simulation.

Trade-offs

The most ownable and the most fun, and the furthest from Heron. Risk: playful can read as less serious to a CFO, and the interaction must stay optional so the message never depends on it.

Mood references

3 · Assay

WebGLEvolves the Orrery and Transmutation work

Idea. The name, taken seriously. Alchemists failed to make gold; assayers succeeded at something more useful, testing metal and certifying it with hallmarks. Legacy systems arrive as rough, mismatched ingots. Scroll heats them, they run together, and are recast as one bar stamped with four hallmarks: Discovered, Architected, Built, Validated. Hallmarks give the site a typographic system with real history (maker's mark, standard mark, office mark, date letter).

Crucible #121010 Slag #4A4642 Melt #FF6A1A Assay silver #C8C6C1 Bone #EDE8DF
DisplayBIG SHOULDERS STENCIL
TextInstrument Sans 17/27

Signature moment

The recast. A WebGL scene where ingots soften (signed-distance blend), flow into a mould and set as one bar. The four hallmarks strike in sequence as the process steps are read.

Motion and 3D

The heaviest option: Three.js or the Orrery's hand-written WebGL2, HDR materials, a static poster fallback, and the full scene paused off-screen. It reuses the Transmutation idea and the PR #1 rendering work.

Trade-offs

Most cinematic and closest to the brand name. Risks: “lead into gold” is a familiar metaphor, near-black plus one hot accent is a common look, and it is the hardest to make fast on mid-range phones.

Refinements already folded into As-Built

  • Away from Heron: cool vellum, not warm paper; cobalt and redline, not orange; condensed sentence-case headlines, not all caps; drawings of software systems, not buildings; no loader.
  • Away from the generated look the current site shares: no cream, no serif display, no terracotta; numbering only where the content is a sequence (sheet numbers, revisions).
  • One bold place: the redraft sequence. Everything else stays quiet so it lands.
  • Borrow from Patch Bay: a small “patch it yourself” moment can live in the Custom Integrations sheet, kept optional.
  • Honesty: every drawing keeps a title-block line saying it is an illustration, as the current site already does.

6Redesign strategy

Information architecture

Two divisions, with Client Solutions leading. Systems Integration & Modernization becomes the lead practice, with five service pages. The existing four service pages stay at their current addresses so search rankings and shared links keep working; whether they stay as peers or fold under the new practice is your call.

Home ├─ Client Solutions /client-solutions/ │ ├─ Systems Integration & Modernization /client-solutions/systems-integration-modernization/ │ │ ├─ CRM migrations │ │ ├─ Custom integrations │ │ ├─ ERP connectivity │ │ ├─ Legacy modernization │ │ └─ Custom software │ └─ Also: Strategy & Architecture, Enterprise AI & Automation, Cloud & Data (existing /services/* pages kept) ├─ Industries (4 existing pages, each with its own as-built drawing) ├─ Products & Innovation /products-innovation/ (“Coming soon”, confirmed by David) ├─ How we work (Discover, Architect, Build, Validate as revisions A–D) ├─ About ├─ Start a project brief (existing 3-step flow, restyled as a drawing request) └─ Privacy

Homepage narrative

  1. Recognition. “Give your business the tools to grow.” Your systems, drawn as they run today, with the problems marked. GTA as a trust line beneath.
  2. Diagnosis. Three redlines a mid-market team will know: re-keyed orders, nightly exports, a system only one person understands.
  3. The offer. Five services as sheets in one drawing set.
  4. The method. The redraft, A to D, with your approval at each gate.
  5. Fit. Industries as variants of the same drawing.
  6. Outcomes and assurance. What changes, who does the work, how fast we reply.
  7. Second division. Products & Innovation, briefly.
  8. Action. The project brief, styled as a drawing request.

Motion system

LayerRule
ScrollNative scrolling everywhere. GSAP ScrollTrigger scrubs only the redraft and the sheet set. No smooth-scroll hijack on touch; optional Lenis on desktop only if it tests well.
Line workStrokes draw at a steady pen speed (about 900 px/s), never bounce. Easing is linear for drawing, ease-out for placement.
TextVisible at rest. Annotations may type on once; headlines never wait on animation.
Reverse scrollEvery scrubbed state is a pure function of scroll position, so scrolling up un-drafts exactly. Tested both ways at each breakpoint.
PointerHovering a system box highlights its connections. Touch gets the same on tap. Nothing depends on hover.
Reduced motionRev A and Rev D shown as two still sheets; sheet set becomes a normal list; no scrubbing.
3DNot required. If wanted later, one isometric exploded view in SVG, or a small WebGL scene behind a poster.

Technical approach

  • Framework: Astro, outputting plain static HTML to GitHub Pages through a GitHub Actions build. Components for the title block, sheet and system box; content collections for services and industries so copy lives in one place. Zero JavaScript by default; motion loads as a small island.
  • Libraries: GSAP with ScrollTrigger (free for commercial use since 2025). Inline SVG for every drawing so text stays real, selectable and translatable. No WebGL in the recommended direction.
  • Page transitions: the browser's View Transitions API, so moving between sheets feels continuous, with a plain fallback.
  • Keep: self-hosted OFL fonts, no trackers, structured data, llms.txt, sitemap, the email-based brief.
  • Budgets: homepage JavaScript under 90 KB compressed; Largest Contentful Paint under 2.0 s on a mid-range phone; layout shift under 0.05; headline readable in the first frame; WCAG 2.2 AA.
  • Verification: Playwright screenshots at 390, 768, 1440 and 1920 wide, forward and reverse scroll stops, reduced motion, keyboard path through the brief, Lighthouse and axe on every pull request. PR #1 already contains a headless screenshot driver we can reuse.

Gate plan

  • Gate 2: full homepage storyboard at desktop and phone, the design system (tokens, type scale, drawing components, motion rules), and one inner page (Systems Integration & Modernization). Built as artboards, shared for review.
  • Gate 3: implementation on a branch in stages (shell and content, then drawings, then the redraft), each as a draft pull request.
  • Gate 4: browser verification on desktop and mobile, both scroll directions, accessibility and performance report, then your approval to publish.
