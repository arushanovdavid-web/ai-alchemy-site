# Critic review: homepage messaging (Experience Spine build)

Reviewed 2026-11-24 against `index.html` in this worktree, `docs/copy/COPY-DECK.md`, `PRODUCT.md`, `llms.txt` and the published homepage (`main:index.html`). Copy only; markup and visuals not judged.

## 1. Verdict

The Spine itself is the strongest messaging this site has had: six chapters that match the drawing, stay inside approved facts and carry the central idea from "four systems, none connected" to "one connected operation". The page around it only partly meets the brief: the lead service and "architecture-first" never reach the first screen, the hero says nothing about who it is for, and after the Spine the page tells the same story three more times (problem grid, service index, method) instead of moving the buyer forward. Show it to the owner as a strong draft with the four decisions in section 3 and edits 1 to 4 below applied first; not as final copy.

## 3. Claims audit

Lines that state or imply something not supported by `PRODUCT.md`, `llms.txt` or the published site:

1. **"Technology consulting and engineering"** (figure label). `llms.txt`, JSON-LD and About say "consulting and implementation" / "consultancy"; "engineering" appears nowhere on `main`. Owner must pick one term, then align JSON-LD and `llms.txt`.
2. **"Products & Innovation is a separate division from our client work."** No source beyond the brief. Unconfirmed what is separate (people, budget or only the work); it also sits awkwardly next to "one team" repeated five times. Copywriter flagged it too.
3. **"In development" stamp** and **"Division II"** label. Both go further than the owner line "taking shape": one implies active build work, the other an org structure the site never states.
4. **"CRM and ERP integrations and migrations" / "move you to one that fits".** "ERP" and platform migration appear nowhere on the published site. Needs the owner to confirm this is work the team has done.
5. **"AI automation and intelligent workflows."** Not a factual claim, but generic AI marketing language the brief excludes.
6. **"The tools you started with now work as one system"** (chapter 6). Reads as a result. The outcome note keeps it honest only if the two always stay together (static and reduced-motion views included). It also contradicts chapter 3, where the old core is replaced, not kept.
7. **"Built for established businesses."** "Established" and "mid-market" are undefined (`PRODUCT.md`: open). Acceptable as is; do not add size bands.
8. **"Our lead service."** Not false, but it is the firm's internal framing. The deck's "Where most projects start" was correctly not used; keep it out unless the owner confirms it.
9. **Share card (out of scope, flagging):** `og:image:alt` still says "Custom software and AI for your business, built in-house", the old lead. The social card will contradict the new positioning until the image and alt change.

Supported and fine: one-business-day reply, free first conversation with David Arushanov, in-house/no outsourcing, manager approval sentence, "We build in stages", "you see the plan and the trade-offs", "teams work from shared information". (`PRODUCT.md` says named team members are unpublished; that is stale, the name is on `main`.)

| 4 | AI core in glass vessel, documents on conveyor, manager's barrier | "The core handles the repeatable steps"; manager decides at the barrier | Matches, but "the core" is undefined and, one chapter after "the old core waits to be replaced", reads as "AI replaces your legacy system". Pattern: chapters 3, 5 and 6 open by narrating the drawing ("A new module goes into the rack", "The line runs under the racks", "Every line is lit"). The reader can see that. Each of those sentences should carry the business meaning instead; the drawing already does the narration.

After the Spine the page restarts rather than continues. Chapter 6 ends at "one connected operation, start a brief", and the next section opens "Architecture first. Then the build." (the Spine's chapters 2 and 3 again), then six problem cards of which 04 to 06 repeat services, then the four services the Spine just walked through, then a four-step method that repeats chapters 2 to 4 nearly verbatim. The natural continuation after the Spine is: what the lead offer covers in detail (integration, CRM/ERP, legacy), who it is for, what Products & Innovation is, how to start. Sheets B and C are the ones that break the thread.

## 5. Eight most valuable edits, ranked

**1. Put positioning and audience in the hero text, not the figure label.** Add an eyebrow above the H1 (the deck planned H-K there; the build moved it to the plate).
- Before: (no eyebrow; figure label "Technology consulting and engineering · Greater Toronto Area")
- After: eyebrow "Architecture-first technology consulting and engineering · Mid-market organizations in the GTA"
- Why: the owner's H1 says nothing about what, for whom or where. This fixes brief bullets 2 and 3 on the first screen without touching the H1. ("Engineering" pending claim 1.)

**2. Make the lead offer carry the Spine's thread and cut the duplicated cards.** Keep problem cards 01 to 03 (Integration, CRM/ERP, Legacy modernization) under the lead; drop 04 to 06 (they repeat Sheet B).
- Before: six cards, three of which restate Custom Software, AI and Architecture.
- After: three cards, all Systems Integration & Modernization.
- Why: makes the lead service visibly deeper than the other four, which is what "lead with" means, and removes the first restart.

**3. Collapse Sheet B to an index line.**
- Before: "Four kinds of work. One team." + "One team carries each from the first conversation to a working system." + four cards + "See all four services".
- After: one line under Sheet A: "Strategy & Architecture · Custom Software & Modernization · Enterprise AI & Automation · Cloud & Data. See all four services." (each name linked)
- Why: the Spine chapters 2 to 5 already explain and link each service; the H2 and intro also say "one team" twice in two lines. The copywriter recommended this as the first cut.

**4. Name the AI in chapter 4.**
- Before: "The core handles the repeatable steps as work comes down the line."
- After: "AI handles the repeatable steps as work comes down the line."
- Why: "the core" is drawing jargon, and following "The old core waits to be replaced" it implies AI replaces your legacy system.

**5. Fix chapter 3's headline and the triple "replace".**
- Before: "Replace what holds you back. / Keep what works." + "A new module goes into the rack. The old core waits to be replaced. We build in stages, so you don't have to replace everything at once."
- After: "Modernize what holds you back. / Keep what works." + "New software goes in beside what still works, and the old system is retired in stages. You don't have to replace everything at once."
- Why: "Modernize" ties the chapter to the lead service; the body explains the picture in business terms and says "replace" once.

**6. Make chapter 6 consistent with chapter 3.**
- Before: "Every line is lit. The tools you started with now work as one system: planned and built by one in-house team, approved by yours."
- After: "Connected, modernized or rebuilt, your systems are designed to work as one: planned and built by one in-house team, approved by yours."
- Why: drops picture narration, stops claiming the original tools survive, and "designed to" frames the outcome as intent, matching the outcome note below it.

**7. Give the lead body an antecedent and drop the internal label.**
- Before: label "Our lead service" / "Another application won't necessarily fix it."
- After: label "Where to begin" / "Another application won't necessarily fix disconnected systems."
- Why: "it" has nothing to refer to under a service-name heading; "Our lead service" is seller language. "Where to begin" invites without claiming a pattern.

**8. Replace the generic AI term.**
- Before: "AI automation and intelligent workflows"
- After: "AI-assisted workflows" (if card 05 survives edit 2; otherwise apply in `/services/`)
- Why: "intelligent workflows" is the AI marketing vocabulary the brief rules out; the body already says "AI-assisted steps".

## 6. Repetition and cuts

- **Outcome note** appears verbatim in chapter 6 and under the method. Keep the chapter 6 copy (it guards the outcome line); cut the method copy.
- **Method (Sheet C)** restates chapter 2 ("You see the plan and the trade-offs before anything is built", verbatim) and chapter 3 ("We build in stages"). Only Validate adds anything new ("Your team tests it on real scenarios"). Either cut the section and move that sentence into chapter 6, or keep only the H2 plus Validate.
- **"One team" / "in-house"**: hero, chapter 6, Sheet A intro, Sheet B H2 and intro, reassurance 1, footer twice. Three uses (hero, chapter 6, reassurance) are enough.
- **Approval sentence** ("technical, pricing, safety and production actions") appears in chapter 4 and reassurance 3. Keep both only if the method section goes; otherwise cut the chapter 4 copy to "Your managers keep the final say."
- **Products & Innovation** names itself three times in four lines (eyebrow, H2, body) and says "our own products" twice. Suggested: H2 "Our own products." body "Separate from our client work, our own products are taking shape. More to share soon." (pending claim 2), stamp removed.
- **Who we work with**: "we don't limit our work to them" and "Your industry isn't listed?" say the same thing. Keep the second; it carries the honest fit offer.
- **Hero microcopy** "Three short steps" sits beside a link to a six-chapter story and above a four-step method; say what has three steps: "The brief takes three short steps. No technical spec needed."
- **"Start a project brief"** appears five times in the body. Fine for a CTA, but the chapter 6 link and the lead button are 1 screen apart; keep both only if Sheet B is collapsed (edit 3).
